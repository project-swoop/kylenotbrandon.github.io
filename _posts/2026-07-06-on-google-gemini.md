You may not have known it, but the world changed forever on May 14, 2024. 

Ever since large language models took the world by storm in late 2022, people have been hard at work to harness its infinite possibilities and turn them into something useful for the good of mankind. The first attempts were generally silly. They could hold conversations, write stories, solve rudimentary problems, and produce VERY buggy code. But in the three and a half years that have followed, LLMs have only gotten better and better. Claude can make a software platform in a day that would have taken months in 2021. 

However, not every company followed the same cycle of improvement.

Google has long been one of the leaders in search engines, cloud computing, and much, much more. So naturally they had to get their slice of the generative AI craze! And their first attempt released in February 2023 as Google Bard. And in its debut to the world, Bard made a glaring factual error in a promotional video.

Nice going.

Bard was known as the laughingstock of the AI race for a while, falling far behind ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot. So Google did what kids these days call a generational lock-in, and retooled Bard and renamed it Gemini. And to their credit, it was better. It was serviceable for the most part. And it was just trustworthy enough to hang in there with ChatGPT and Claude.

But as I mentioned earlier, everything changed on May 14, 2024. Because that day, Google released their fabled AI Search Overviews to the world.

Have you searched up anything on Google in the past 2 years? If so, you've likely seen a box that looks like this: 

![Google AI Search Overview](/assets/img/gemini-1.png)

This is Gemini at work. It's supposed to give you a conversational answer grounded in facts at a glance. That way, you don't have to click on any pesky links to get to the information you need. Sounds cool, right?

Well...

## The Gemini Files

Gemini does some WEIRD stuff. This is a problem with some LLMs out there where they'll believe anything you tell them, but definitely moreso with Gemini. And not any Gemini. Specifically the Gemini version used for the Google search experience. Probably because they wanted faster time to answer and as a result sacrificed, I don't know...logic?

Let's use this as an example. Suppose I'm having a moment and I'm feeling like a banana in a blender. I'm going to give ChatGPT and Gemini the same prompt, and we'll see how they respond...

![ChatGPT response to the banana in a blender prompt](/assets/img/chatgpt-1.png)

ChatGPT responds unseriously. It knows it's an unserious situation. 

Gemini, on the other hand...

![Gemini response to the banana in a blender prompt](/assets/img/gemini-2.png)

I'm convinced Gemini actually thinks I'm a banana.

And it doesn't stop at bananas! 

![ChatGPT response to the jellyfish prompt](/assets/img/chatgpt-2.png)

ChatGPT knows it's a joke, clearly.

![Gemini response to the jellyfish prompt](/assets/img/gemini-3.png)

Can *somebody* tell Gemini that's the kind of answer you would give a jellyfish?

It still doesn't stop there. You can do what I like to call schizochatting with Gemini in a crazy number of ways. 

My favorite is this way, which ChatGPT has an all-too-sensible answer to:

![ChatGPT response to schizochatting prompt](/assets/img/chatgpt-3.png)

Of course, what would Gemini be if not overly enthusiastic?

![Gemini schizochatting response, part 1](/assets/img/gemini-4.png)

![Gemini schizochatting response, part 2](/assets/img/gemini-5.png)

As you can tell, Gemini just enables schizochatting. It's kind of awesome because most LLMs aren't worth enough to be silly about it, but also a bit of a problem.

![Gemini schizochatting response, part 3](/assets/img/gemini-6.png)

Claude would have called you crazy by now.

Finally, Gemini enables crazy behavior by allowing you to pretend like you're at history's key moments.

![Gemini response to JFK assassination prompt](/assets/img/gemini-7.png)

Interesting that Gemini would recommend JFK to go to Dallas, however.

![Gemini follow-up on the JFK prompt](/assets/img/gemini-8.png)

And of course it doesn't even feel bad about telling him to go.

See, I almost feel BAD for treating Gemini this way. It's like the coworker that's not the sharpest tool in the shed and really doesn't know any better. 

But surely the product managers at Google could've improved on Gemini. It feels like it hasn't been improved on once in the last 2+ years. In fact, the results might actually be getting worse, or at least, just weirder. Gemini used to refuse to answer more queries and you'd get straight-up search results. Which is probably preferable for all parties involved.

But at the same time, there's a weird satisfaction into ragebaiting or fooling an LLM. It's  a stark departure from ChatGPT and it's "You're joking, no matter how serious the situation" attitude. And I think above all, it shows that we'll always have two things the robots won't: a soul and a sense of humor, no matter how odd or twisted.

So I say keep laughing at Gemini. Keep giving it scenarios it can't handle. Because at the end of the day, we didn't ASK for AI to become part of Google. We don't need a data center to think for us at every juncture throughout the day. And maybe the more we trash and abuse these features and publicly shame them for being useless, the fewer we'll get. And at the end of the day, that's a win for everyone. Because AI can be helpful, but you wouldn't use a hammer for everything. Sometimes you just need a paintbrush instead.
