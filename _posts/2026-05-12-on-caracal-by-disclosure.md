Over the past two years, I’ve craved stimulation. 

And by and large, I’ve been able to fill that void in my life with electronic music. I always liked it as a kid but I got back into it the last couple years of college and have REALLY learned to love it again in my professional life. 

I regret feeling like electronic music was unsophisticated and childish. I really do. I think I paid too much attention to judgmental voices when it came to music – the “if it’s not an indie artist with 1M or fewer streams I’m not interested,” crowd.

Big regrets. Because electronic music as a whole RIPS.

And I could talk about the super energetic stuff - all the way from the more common house to the wild and wonderful (to some) worlds of dubstep, hardstyle, and industrial. 

But if you’re familiar with *Caracal* by Disclosure, you would know this is a bit more downtempo. It’s a bit more synth-pop or deep house, with slower, more measured undertones compared to its predecessor, *Settle*. If *Settle* is an espresso martini, *Caracal* is a classic old fashioned with a good bourbon. It’s darker. It’s more polished. It’s more mature. It’s ready to wind down at a reasonable hour.

But silly cat-name aside, *Caracal* is SUCH a great vibe for me. It’s the, *“I just finished a hard, 10-hour Thursday at work”* album. It’s the, *”I’m about to be on a plane for 6 hours so might as well hit up the lounge and have a couple to smooth the ride out”* album. It’s the, *”I’m gonna walk around the city and talk to NOBODY and go into a bar and get a drink and walk out without elaborating”* album.

And unfortunately, it’s criminally underrated and overshadowed by its big brother in *Settle*. Where *Caracal* drives the blacked-out Mercedes that’s classy and comfortable and may even have champagne flutes in the back, *Settle* shows up to the valet in a Porsche 911 in electric blue. And probably confuses *Caracal*’s car for a black cab.

But every song sounds great and, at least in my opinion, this album has NO skips. Is any song groundbreaking? Not really. I mean, “Magnets” having Lorde on it is probably as avant-garde as it gets. But they get the job *done.* Sometimes it’s the old adage of “Good is easy to notice. Greatness is sometimes so subtle you don’t even know it’s there.” (Yes, I probably did butcher that saying.)

But where, oh where, have we seen something like this before…
## Carrick-al 

This is gonna take another unexpected turn. If any of you are big English soccer fans, and you’re of a certain vintage, you may (should?) remember longtime Manchester United and occasional England midfielder Michael Carrick. Number 16, he played a deep-lying, defensive and playmaking role for about a decade in the heart of the Man United midfield. He had the likes of Ryan Giggs, Wayne Rooney, and Cristiano Ronaldo in front of him. He had Paul Scholes next to him. And he had Rio Ferdinand and Nemanja Vidic behind him. 

So even at his club, he was frequently surrounded by flashier players or bigger names, and his brilliance was really only ever recognized when he *wasn’t* on the pitch. He wasn’t fast. He wasn’t wildly athletic. He wouldn’t sprint half the length of the pitch. But he rarely made mistakes.

Other defensive midfielders like Owen Hargreaves, Daley Blind, and regrettably, a pretty poor Morgan Schneiderlin and an aging Bastian Schweinsteiger would come in and misplay passes, drop defensive assignments, and cause fans worldwide to groan at their televisions. They’d flock to Twitter and say how they never had a bad word to say about Michael Carrick in all their years. But they’d soon as easily forget his brilliance. 

It was even worse for England. These two guys named Steven Gerrard and Frank Lampard and the English reliance on the 4-4-2 frequently kept Carrick out of the lineup. Which we’ll never know of the ramifications of that. Could Michael Carrick have led England to a World Cup if he was given the full confidence, more caps, and a 4-3-3? Probably not. But we’ll never get to know!

And this is why I think Michael Carrick and *Caracal* are similar. Their time has come and gone. Neither broke ground or shattered earth. But they didn’t do much wrong. At no point do either fall flat. And in an era of inconsistent, flashy, and attention-seeking players, and works of art, that’s something I reach we could all use a little more of. 

So if you find yourself in a hotel lobby or airport lounge or other location where you may take it upon yourself to feel sophisticated (but not in a stuffy way), try *Caracal.* And if you hate it, let me know at [kylenotbrandon@gmail.com](mailto:kylenotbrandon@gmail.com).

See ya!
