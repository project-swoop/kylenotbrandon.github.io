County fairs are a spectacle that Americans travel far and wide for. But *should* they?

I’ve gone to the same county fair 3 years in a row, and I’ve come away with some opinions on them. They’re in some ways a representation of America in its truest form – agriculture, farming, etc., and in some ways a representation of a caricature of America – excess, grandiosity, and loudness. But it’s where two very interesting subsections of the population meet. And it mirrors language I’ve been hearing recently as part of a certain trend...

## America A and America B
I’ve been seeing the US divided into “America A” and “America B” a whole lot recently, and it’s gotten me thinking quite a bit. America A represents the wealthy, urbanized, educated, and typically coastal regions of the US, while America B refers to the rural, agrarian, working-class parts of the country. 

To me, America B is clearly meant as a dig at rural, Republican America. And while I do think that a lot of the Republican Party is full of immoral, shallow, feckless cowards, this so-called America B is also full of hardworking, good, honest people. Conversely, while America A might be morally upstanding, they’re their own brand of shallow, performative, hapless people at times. Neither one should put themselves over the other (it’s likely in the grand scheme of things we’re all equally as bad as each other), but as a resident of this so-called America A, it’s clearly a term being used to generate animosity towards a perceived lesser class of people. Which is wrong, even if urban America does clear rural America by quite a bit.

But really this has little to do with the topic of today’s blog, so let me get back on track here. What is relevant, however, is that America A usually perceives county fairs as a spectacle – a glimpse into a life they don’t live and don’t have to live. America B usually views county fairs as a celebration and manifestation of the life they’ve worked so hard to cultivate and keep. (There’s also the America C that runs all the rides at the county fair, but I won’t dare to get into that right now.)

I must admit that at the county fair, I do feel a bit like a visitor to another planet. Living in the DC area, the population that attends this county fair that’s located rather far out from the city is very much a group of people I don’t interact with often. But DC has been no stranger to the opposite happening recently, with all sorts of Freedom 250 (ugh) events taking over the city. It’s honestly a pretty big culture shock either way. People are louder, less reserved, more extroverted, and in many respects, having more fun. But that’s a bit of the beauty of the county fair in the sense that *anyone* could reasonably have a good time going to one. 

But let’s go through what the county fair has to offer, and along the way I’ll offer my unsolicited opinion on each aspect of these unique celebrations of local agriculture and community.

## The rides
The carnival midway is the largest part of the county fair, no doubt. Its dozens of trailers and eclectic mix of employees travel across the country as part of a traveling show, unfold themselves into actual rides, and entertain the masses for a few days every year. 

This is the sketchiest part of the fair by far. I am NOT a roller coaster fan by a long shot, and I’m even moreso unnerved when I see, or heaven forbid get on, a ride whose last inspection was 2003, for all I know. I’ve seen zip ties, speed tape, and just about every temporary mechanical fix in the book, and they all seem to find their way onto county fair rides. It is genuinely a scary experience, compounded by my social media algorithm’s penchant for recommending me the kind of videos where the pirate ship ride unbolts itself and a mass casualty event unfolds. I’m shuddering right now even thinking about it, jeez.

## The food
Now this is something I can get behind.

Fair food is fried, fun, and on a stick. Unlike the rides, it WILL, not may, kill you given the chance. 

Ever thought you could go your whole life without knowing what a fried Oreo would taste like? Think again! Have you ever dismissed a gallon of lemonade as being too big for a cup and a straw? Think again! Thought you would go your whole life without having to wonder if there’s real meat in that giant smoked turkey leg? That’s right, think again!!!

Fair food is one of those things you get once a year, get overcharged for it, eat it, and then have a positive-enough experience to the point you’d have it again. And there’s nothing wrong with that at all. I enjoy eating like a chud every once in a while. Everyone should! Just don’t convince yourself it’s good for you. 

## The entertainment
Fairs have plenty of forms of entertainment. Show animal competitions. Dogs doing tricks. Live bands. Controversially, kids having their own…pageants? (Weird.) But the best thing I’ve ever seen at a county fair, and my favorite part, is the demolition derby. 

Now I like motorsport, and I fully accept that’s probably a red flag for being a chug form of sport. But demolition derbies are just so…watchable. Once it’s in motion, you CAN’T bear to take your eyes off it. Just a bunch of painfully white guys driving their cars into each other in a mudpit. The gasoline smell. The carnage. The flames. The fact the drivers get mad without fail. It’s truly peak entertainment, and I highly recommend you all go to experience one for yourself.

There are monster trucks, and Elvis impersonators, and all other sorts of entertainment run by amateurs. It’s genuinely fun to see folks just go out there and do their best at something, no matter the context. They really do put everything into it, and it’s one of the reasons I recommend you go to a county fair. Support people for being talented!

## The petting zoo
I think petting zoos are gross. And I feel bad for the animals. Imagine being an animal, being in a gross little enclosure, and having little child hands sticking out to touch you all day. Maybe, just maybe, they feed you. That’s sad! It’s summer, it’s like 100 degrees outside, and nobody’s having THAT much fun. (Also sheep and goats freak me out.) Enough said here.

## The booths
This one is also a mixed-to-negative bag. Some of the booths are imperceptible and you can walk right past them, like the one sponsored by the bank, or the fire department, or the police. Some you’d rather not talk to, like the political parties. Some aren’t applicable to you, like the people trying to sell you an HVAC system. And some are just selling crocheted, dropshipped trinkets, and nothing more. 

But the one booth that gets me every time are the churches. I guess I look approachable. And I guess I’m friendly in public. But three years in a row the pastor at the Baptist church has asked me if I’m going to heaven, and I’ve given a noncommittal answer, and I’ve been roped into a conversation against my will. And I’ve been prayed for. And even this past fair I was told I was born again, so now I’m a born-again Christian I guess.

I know it’s meant from a place of genuine good, and these folks are trying to evangelize people. And I’m not mad at that. Moreso I’m mad at (1) their tactics, and (2) me for being kind enough to play along with them. But alas. 

## Conclusion
There is a lot to love about the county fair. It’s diverse in ways you may not expect, and it’s a celebration of what makes rural communities so special and the backbone of America.

And I’m gonna go out on a limb and say that you, dear reader, are likely part of the urban, educated "America A” (God that’s a really unsavory term to keep saying. How derogatory to the other half of the country). And I recommend that you experience a county fair. You don’t have to love it. You can take it at face value. But you should respect the fact that to some people, this is the Super Bowl. This is their everything. They’ve been waiting the whole year for this. And it shows. The work into cultivating crops, caring for livestock, building a demolition derby car, and whatever else goes on at the fair takes a ton of coordination, a ton of effort, and a ton of time. So go. Experience it. Drop any premonitions you may have. And have a good time.
