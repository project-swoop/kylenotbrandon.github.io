## Motivation
What gets you up out of bed in the morning?

Well first and foremost, it’s probably your job (if you’re employed). Unfortunately I fall into the 62% of Americans that have to work to pay bills and live a life and so on. Not that I’m complaining about that – I get to live a life of relative comfort because I have a job. But it’s certainly the thing that wakes me up at 6:30 every weekday. (Except today, when I get to fly to California to see my family.)

But aside from the things we have to do, we get up and out of the house for a multitude of reasons. And oftentimes, these things we choose to do are effortful endeavors. We call things labors of love and we invest time, money, and work into them – without expecting much in return. Normally, these things would be considered a bad deal…and we *wouldn’t* partake in them. But we do, thanks to the wonderful phenomenon called motivation.

I normally Google definitions, but today I’m going to try defining motivation in my own words. Motivation is the cause or reason for a person doing something they would not do in the absence of said reason. We do have motivations on a couple levels – I don’t have the motivation to die or become homeless, so I both breathe and eat (for the former) and go to my job (for the latter). 

But aside from the things we do to survive, we have the motivation to do things that help us thrive. We have hobbies. We work out. We read. We learn. We pursue interests. But why? What makes things so compelling to us? And contrarily, why do we **stop** doing things?

## What I’m motivated to do
I think I’ve covered this multiple times on this blog, but I’m one of those people who’s a bit restless. I’m often discontent with letting the world pass me by without discovering it. If I’m in a new city/location, it’s only right to explore. I’m motivated to do this, I would say. 

Why, exactly, am I motivated to do this? I’m not sure. But I think I’ve had this attitude ever since I was a kid. 

#### An embarrassing story
When I was little, I used to wake up every day at 6-6:30. Yes, that includes weekends.

And if I woke up any later than that, I would be MAD. Like actually very upset with myself, because at that point I had “wasted” part of the day. Never mind we lived somewhere wholly unexciting where sleeping in 30 minutes literally affected nothing. So it’s a bit weird and irrational that I do this, it seems.

Another thing that I’ve been motivated to do recently, as you all know, is [run.](https://kylenotbrandon.blog/blog/2026/07/05/on-running/) And it’s high time I bring something up.

I…don’t think I’m motivated by my disgust for other runners anymore. 

I really don’t know what changed within me, genuinely. But 1. I’ve never been more motivated to run and 2. I suddenly don’t feel ill will towards run clubbers anymore (as I did right around a month and a half ago.) I genuinely do think my motivation to run is now *fully* driven by the urge to improve myself and to become healthy again. (Maybe because my birthday is coming up and I’m once again confronted with the fact I’ll never be as young as I am today, but I digress...)

But I am running a ton more, and more effectively too. I’ve run 2 10Ks this week, something I legitimately have never done in my life – even once. And I *do* think the motivation is fully from within. But that’s not to say people annoying me doesn’t motivate me for something new-ish to me...

## The gym
Guys. What are we doing???

I’ve always been too self-conscious to go to the gym regularly. Mostly because I thought I would look out of place compared to the other people in the gym. But now, I think other gym-goers should be put to shame. 

It’s happened on multiple occasions where I go to the gym and…some people are really NOT doing anything on those machines. Most notably, I wanted to use a bench. The nice bench in our gym. But someone had brought their freaking iPad into the gym and was drawing notes while doing one rep every 2-3 minutes.

Another notable occasion where this happened was Sunday night (if I recall correctly). I arrived at about 9:30 and was the only person there (which is fine by me because at that point, nobody’s perceiving me). About 10 minutes later, this guy walks in and promptly sits down on a leg machine. And proceeds to do NOTHING for the next 30 minutes. 

This guy was sitting, scrolling his phone, and doing 5 reps every 5 minutes, I swear. I got through a full workout in that time!

Me being me and being ever the optimist (sarcasm, in case that doesn’t come through over a website), I conjectured that maybe he just needed to leave his apartment for a bit because he had an argument and just wanted to be somewhere else. Which may have explained the FLIP-FLOPS in the gym. But it was annoying – and annoying enough to the point where it motivated ME to push harder. Which was appreciated, I guess.

It’s like how we learn as many lessons watching people do the right thing as watching people do the wrong things. Or so I’m told. Negative reinforcement, in a way – because then you’re not “that guy."

But there’s one last type of motivation I want to cover. We’ve gone through intrinsic motivation (driven by you), motivation by comparison, and then…extrinsic motivation. Which is my least favorite. 

## When others motivate you too hard
I don’t know if this is uniquely a me experience, but this is a really hard one for me.

When I was a kid, it felt like I would take to something. Like reading, for example. I would go to the library, check out a book, and love it. I LOVED reading as a kid. 

But then I would tell my mom about it. She would say, “oh, that’s great!” and we would then go get the sequel to said book, or a similar book. But then my mom would keep asking about what I was reading, and keep pushing me to read more…and at some point I just lost motivation to. Because at that point the motivation wasn’t coming from me anymore. It felt like I was reading because my mom kept asking me how it was going, or why I wasn’t reading more. 

And maybe this speaks to more of the pressure I was put under as a kid. I always had to have good grades. I always had to keep learning. And some of it, I did want to do on my own. But my parents kept pressuring me into it, and asking me how it was going…and at some point, it does get tiresome. 

That’s not to say that it wasn’t helpful. Because it was. I got to go to a great school and now I have a great career and a great life because my parents pushed me to study and learn. But it never fully felt like I drove it myself.

It was the same way in soccer, or karate, or when I bought my first bass guitar…and maybe it says more about me than my parents, but it just felt like I wasn’t doing things at the pace they wanted. So why do them?

So if there are any parents reading this (which I doubt there are), encourage your kids. Encourage their interests. Make sure they do well in school (I always thought that was excusable, and I understood why my parents wanted me to do well in school). But PLEASE make sure to give your kids space to evaluate their own motivation levels for certain hobbies/interests. Let them take the joy from those experiences. You want them to do well, yes – but you want them to live their own lives at the same time. And I haven’t had much greater joy than doing things for myself for the first time in a long time as an adult.
