I…think I like cooking?

I mean, I love baking. I’m also better at baking than I am at cooking, so I might have the preference towards baking, but cooking isn’t all that bad. Until it gets to a point...

It’s 6 PM. You just get home from work. You change, you work out, you get back home. Now it’s 7:30 or 8 o’clock.

Do you *really* want to cook at this hour? Probably not...

Which is why I’m here to vouch for the surprisingly simple wonder that is the Crock Pot. A Crock Pot, you can feasibly do the prep for on a Sunday, put in the fridge, turn on Monday morning, and come home to dinner that’s ready Monday evening. It’s awesome!

But I found out that many peers of my generation despise – or even *dread* – seeing the Crock Pot sitting out on the counter. Why, that is?

Well, let me say that it’s likely not the fault of Mr. Pot. It’s their parents’ fault. 

Before I go on too deep of a tangent, let me take you back in time here. In the halcyon days of February 2025, we had a chili cook-off at work because our coworker (not from America) had never had chili before. And I wanted to enter (because I fancied myself an alright cook). And what does one need to cook chili all day at the office? A Crock Pot. So he judged, and we made chili, and I chose [this recipe](https://sallysbakingaddiction.com/my-favorite-slow-cooker-chicken-chili-recipe/). 

Now, you may be thinking, and I get it.

“Sally’s **Baking** Addiction? Cream cheese? You don’t know ball!"

But I do. This chili was SO good. I recommend you try that recipe if you’re looking for something easy and good. It’s still one of my favorites in the Crock Pot. Make it as spicy (or unspicy) as you want. And the cream cheese melts away…and it’s incredible. 

Unfortunately due to the incessant complaints of my (now former) coworker who insisted chili without ground meat is not chili, I finished third. Out of three. But guess who didn’t get fired from their job for creating a toxic workplace environment…that’s right.

(I walked past chili hater a couple neighborhoods over yesterday. We ignored each other.)

I left defeated, but my $20 Target Crock Pot remains a staple in my house. And at the request of a dear reader (shoutout Julka!), today I’ll be walking you through other recipes I love in the Crock Pot…and one flop.

## Year 2 Chili

Undeterred after my chili flop, I tried again this past February. Crock Pot in hand (well, in seat. I drove to the office), I came back with a vengeance. 

Conventional wisdom would dictate learning from my errors of the past and bringing a more traditional chili to the contest. Of course I did not follow that logic. 

This year’s chili was a labor of love and dedicated to my father’s homeland of New Mexico. And if you’re unfamiliar, I made green chili…stew. [Recipe here.](https://iamafoodblog.com/this-new-mexico-colorado-hatch-chile-verde-recipe-may-the-best-pork-stew-youve-ever-tasted/)

Now this went really well – I was super happy with the way it turned out. Roasted my own chiles and tomatillos, browned the pork, went all over town for the right ingredients. And day of, my chili tasted the best. My competitor’s chili was also quite good. But mine tasted better and he even admitted as much. 

But…mine had pork in it and thus due to religious reasons many prospective voters could not taste my chili. And thus I came second (out of four). A paltry improvement. I have the silver spoon (I know, make your jokes here) trophy sitting in my kitchen as a reminder that whatever you’re about to eat in my kitchen is probably good, but there’s always a better cook out there. Oh well.

But beyond chilis...

## HANDS!!! I NEED HANDS!!!

I have quite the affinity for Chicago. It’s got a character to it that I’ve only ever experienced there and in Philadelphia. People are proud of a city despite its flaws and it shows. Maybe because the people who live there live there for longer and are thus more invested? I’m not sure.

But anyway, Chicago has its fair share of culture, and culinary culture as well. While you can’t cook a Chicago dog or a pizza in a Crock Pot–actually shit, my sources are telling me you CAN in theory cook [a pizza in a Crock Pot](https://slowcookermeals.com/crock-pot-pizza/)–ignore that for now–you can certainly cook [Italian beef.](https://www.spendwithpennies.com/crock-pot-italian-beef-sandwiches/)

Which is actually the most recent thing I made in my Crock Pot! It came out really well – lot of authentic flavor and I bought extra sliced pepperoncinis and giardiniera to put on top of the sandwiches. The only thing with this recipe is the beef is shredded rather than sliced, but the flavor is largely the same as you’d find in any beef shop in Chicago. Highly recommend for the curious and the homesick. 

## Bless your heart

Chicken and dumplings is a southern classic. You can find it at the authentic places like Cracker Barrel, which is how you can really tell it’s authentic. (Sorry, lol.)

Uh, anyway. Chicken and dumplings is a Crock Pot classic and super good…if you can find the ingredients for it.

I had a whale of a time trying to find cream of celery. I could not do so. I went to three grocery stores and none of them had cream of freaking celery. I subbed in cream of mushroom and just added celery but…I don’t know if that does it.

The dumplings are undoubtedly the best part, because you just buy biscuit dough and put it on top and eat it while it’s still sort of doughy and goopy. And man it’s good. I use [this recipe](https://www.spendwithpennies.com/crock-pot-chicken-and-dumplings/#wprm-recipe-container-121641), but my parents have a different (albeit very similar) one and they both hit every time. Now I kind of want to buy the ingredients next time I go grocery shopping. It is that serious.

## …did you say brisket?

YES! You can make brisket in a Crock Pot. 

I will preface this by saying it’s not incredible, but you know what? It’s a lot better than you would think. You cook it while making the sauce for it, which is fun. I think my biggest struggle (putting the [recipe here](https://www.recipetineats.com/slow-cooker-beef-brisket-with-bbq-sauce/) before I forget to write it down later) is finding the right cut of meat for it. None of them have been perfect, and perhaps I could also stand to lengthen the cook time on it a bit. Despite all this, it’s still a fun option for something unique you really wouldn’t think of as a Crock Pot dish.

## Fancy stuff

[Beef bourguignon.](https://therecipecritic.com/slow-cooker-beef-bourguignon/) Try this in a Crock Pot and it will blow your mind. Easily the best meal I’ve made all year. I don’t even have to say any more than that.

## Mongolian Beef

I sort of forgot about this one and that I made it. It felt a bit weird coating beef in cornstarch and I didn’t have high hopes, but with well-cooked rice this was a hit. Especially with the green onion bits in there. Recipe is [here](https://www.dinneratthezoo.com/slow-cooker-mongolian-beef/) – and I’m not not elaborating on some of these because they’re less good per se, I just don’t have as much of a story behind them.

But finally, we get to my biggest flop.

## Divorce Me Chicken

So for context, and as a bit of a confession, I picked a handful of these recipes off Reddit. The beef bourguignon and Mongolian beef being prime examples of this, I figured [this marry me chicken recipe](https://www.themagicalslowcooker.com/marry-me-chicken/) would be pretty good. And…it wasn’t. 

I don’t know if it was something I did. I don’t know if it was the ingredients I bought. But for whatever reason, I had a really hard time getting the sauce to form a cohesive, well, sauce, and as a result the texture was weird and I didn’t end up enjoying this one. There also wasn’t as much flavor as I was expecting, which was a bummer. Some folks in the comments/reviews left similar review of this recipe, but it is to date my biggest Crock Pot flop. And hopefully my last one.

## Conclusion

In the course of researching for this blog, I rediscovered some recipes for which I have a great fondness for, reminisced over good times with my trusty $20 kitchen appliance (which has paid for itself many times over now), and even found new recipes in the process. On Reddit, I came across [this Google doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1yFLZUcp1MPscG7cEjyT1OVjsmaNVQ-jMePxnR44Ihck/edit?tab=t.0) which lists far more recipes which I’m excited to try in the future. And maybe even some unconventional ones like cheesecake or pizza are to follow.

But I think the simple humility of the Crock Pot has a lot in the way to teach us about life. Sometimes the things we love and cherish the most aren’t the fanciest. Sometimes they’re uncomplicated, down-to-earth, and always there for you. Sort of like your grandma. So I encourage you, dear reader, to go forth and prep. And 6-8 hours before you’re ready to eat, turn that Crock Pot dial. You won’t regret it. (But you might regret [subscribing to this blog.](https://kylenotbrandon.blog/subscribe/))
