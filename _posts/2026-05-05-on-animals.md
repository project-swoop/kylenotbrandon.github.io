If you were an animal, what animal would you be?

This is a fun question because sometimes in asking it, you likely already have an answer in mind for the person you’re asking it. You’ve probably already transposed their human features onto a faceless animal in some regard. But why do you answer the way you do?

See, a lot of people want to say, “Oh, I’d be a lion” or “a tiger” or “a wolf.” Give it up bro, you’re not *that* cool. 

A lion doesn’t have to tell you it’s a lion (or however the saying goes). But the truth is that whatever animal people say, it’s likely cooler than they are. That’s not an indictment on them, per se–it’s just that lions are like 99.9th percentile cool. 

There is a good argument to be made that nobody in their right mind would compare themselves to something dumb like an insect. “I would be an aphid.” Yeah right. But also a ton of this is personality-based. 

Which leads me to potentially the craziest take on here yet. Hear me out, because I don’t think I’m 100% right on this (as I’m not 100% right on anything). But this question is pretty much an analogue for, and probably best perceived as, “What would your fursona be?”

Be confused. Raise an eyebrow. Be appalled. This is a safe space for that, and I cringe thinking about the parallel too, don’t get me wrong.

But think about this: are furries ever the uncool animals? No, they’re all the ones people think of as cool. (I think.) Nobody’s dressing up as, like, a rat. (Once again, I only conjecture upon this, and I am NOT putting in the effort to find out.)

But also, the ones furries ARE dressing up as are way too cool for the average person. You can’t all be the kings of the jungle (or whatever, idk). 

And that’s enough talk of that for this blog post (and maybe this blog at large).

So in the middle ground of things, what animals ARE we?

The common answers:

## A bear

Once again, a little on the too-cool side, probably. But this would be such a less common answer if bears weren’t human-shaped and did human things like stand on two legs and eat processed food. But I could see it in some cases.

## An otter

This almost works for some people who purport themselves to be harmless and non-threatening and cutesy. The problem is that otters are not harmless and very threatening and only cute at surface level, which raises some issues of contradiction. Or maybe people that say that are actually hiding the skeletons deep in the closet.

## A raccoon 

Still just too humanoid! I do get it though. The raccoon is the person that gets 3 hours a sleep a night and smokes cigarettes and mainlines Monster Energy to get through their day. So an acceptable answer for some. 

## Dogs

This is ignoring the painfully obvious pitfall that while dogs are sociable and friendly and excitable, we love to anthropomorphize and personify dogs in a way that makes them TOO sociable and friendly and excitable to be human. If humans either mapped to dogs or cats, at LEAST 95% of people would map to being cats just on the basis of not treating everyone they meet like their favorite person ever. Or, in the case of other dogs, being outwardly aggressive towards everyone they meet. Although the latter seems a little more common. 

## Wolves

See (a) the above and (b) the further above about you not being cool enough to compare yourself here.

## Dolphins

See, this makes sense! I think dolphins are the bubbliest and most emotionally intelligent you could get and still make a fair comparison, as compared to the aforementioned dogs. But the whole aquatic thing doesn’t really compute as a fair comparison at the same time.

## Birds of any sort

Far too graceful, perhaps. If you’re like an Olympian or athlete, maybe? Maybe the qualifier here is you need to be able to do a backflip or something.

## *Well, you’ve ruled out so many animals. What even is there left?*

See, this gets back to the original crux of this debate. There are a few ways to approach this:

1. You’ve already made up some projection of an animal onto me (or vice versa) in your head, so does it *really* matter what I say? Might as well just have told me, “You remind me of a ______.”
2. You’re going to cast judgment on me if I say something that doesn’t line up with your expectations. 
3. Well, humans are an animal, no? Could I just say that? Well yes, but then I’m a spoilsport (and we can’t have that). 
4. Most people can’t say their favorite animal or the animal they think is the coolest because it’s probably cooler than them.
5. Most people have been asked this before, and they probably already have an answer or have received judgment passed about this. So with all this…
## What animal do I think I would be?

Good question. I can’t say my favorite animal because that would be too cool and I am uncool. I would say bear but bears may be too humanoid by my own standards. Raccoons are also too humanoid and too many people call themselves raccoons. I occasionally live a struggle but not a raccoon-level struggle. 

A couple folks at work said I was honey badger-esque recently, on the logic that honey badgers appear to be cute (which might be a stretch for me) and sweet but harbor a lot of internalized rage (which is NOT a stretch for me). So maybe that. In a similar way, other folks at work have given the same reason but with a Chihuahua instead. Which I immediately reported them to HR for saying. 

But all this to say–I really don’t *know* what I’d compare myself to. I am a human. I am a uniquely human being and therefore that is the animal I am.
