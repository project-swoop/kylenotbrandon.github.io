Everyone’s creeped out by, or scared of, something (or somethings). And if you’re not, you’re lying.

Being creeped out is normal. It’s probably a survival adaptation or something. I can proudly say stuff creeps me out. But there’s a bit of a distinction in my head between being scared by something and being creeped out by something. So let’s touch on that quickly before we go any further.

## Prologue: Being scared of something 

According to Google, my favorite source, being scared of something is an active, immediate fear of known danger. I’m be scared of having a gun pointed at me, or someone grabbing my leg in the ocean, or, in the notable case for me…

### Computer errors

Now I’m young enough to have grown up around computers, but I’m old enough (2002) to have grown up around computers (and electronics at large) back when they still threw out really cryptic and scary errors for no reason. There was once as a kid the Wii froze up while I was playing a Nerf-themed game, and it did that weird thing where the sound clipped the same tenth of a second over and over again, and I LOST it. There was once where our DVD player would keep opening and closing the disc drive, and I was freaked out. But more notably than anything was the damn Windows XP error sound and the [blue screen of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_screen_of_death).

## Creepy stuff

First, I asked a few people what things they find creepy. Here they are:
##### Being alone in a house/apartment
This one can be true, and I’ve felt that way before, but honestly it comes and goes for me. If I know I’m the only one that’s going to be occupying a space for a period of time, nothing. But if it’s something other people can/will enter and I hear a noise, for some reason that sets my brain off. Sort of the opposite of most people maybe – where others assume someone broke in, I assume the house just made an odd noise, as opposed to the other way around. But very real. **Creepy rating: 6/10** 
##### Driving at night with no music
This one I get, but only in specific circumstances. And I’d actually wager no music isn’t the problem. So maybe I don’t fully get it. Driving at night can be a little unsettling sometimes, but if I’m in a populated area or like a suburb, I don’t have a problem with it. Especially not if someone else is driving. If I am, I probably am a little more on edge. But I feel like the case for me is more if I’m driving somewhere rural, or in the middle of nowhere. Nobody’s gonna help you out there no matter the time of day. But that’s music nonwithstanding. **Creepy rating: 4/10**
##### Clowns
Clowns have never really done it for me. Is it the supposed to be childish but so often perverted into a creepy being stereotype? Is it the killer clown trend of 2016? Is it the face paint? To me, clowns are more of an enraging thing. The clown emoji successfully ragebaits me every time without fail. So I think there is another child-oriented thing on this list that’s creepy, but I’ll leave it for later. **Creepy rating: 3/10**
##### Belly buttons
Yes! I hate my belly button. Humans got some of the worst belly buttons, and sometimes I fear that it’ll just sort of fall apart one day and my guts will spill out. It’s also not comforting that the belly button is used as a surgical portal for the abdomen sometimes. Why? Why mutilate something that’s pretty much a giant scar? Additional points if you’re an outie because that is crazy work. I’m feeling queasy thinking about MY belly button right now. **Creepy rating: 8/10**
##### Animatronics
*Five Nights at Freddy’s* is largely sensationalized. But you should still be afraid of animatronics the way you should be of any robot – because as all systems do, robots fail and animatronics are no exception. You need a LOT of torque and power to get one of those things to work, and I can’t imagine what that could do to a kid if something bad happens, either unintentionally or not. **Creepy rating: 7/10**

That’s what I stand on some common creepy things. I’m sure you may think some of these are creepy that I said were not, or vice versa. Interested to hear what you think at [kylenotbrandon@gmail.com](mailto:kylenotbrandon@gmail.com).

Anyway, here are some things that I find creepy:
## Top 5 things I find creepy

I’m really interested in what you people will think of this, because it’s a little different. 
##### Old, tall buildings
Are we so sure that an old building has been properly inspected for structural integrity lately? In college, our engineering building went 4 floors up with 2 basement levels, and I always had a pit in my stomach in the sub-basement level, looking up at the cracks in the concrete above me. I recently stayed in a 14-floor hotel that was built in 1969, and that’s not even as tall or as old as some other buildings that tower over it. But especially with it being in California, what if there’s an earthquake? Did they even build things to code back then? Am I breathing in 60-year-old dust? Have they cleaned the AC units and vents or am I breathing in mold, too? Do the pipes have lead?

I just feel like there’s only so much you can do to keep up with a massive building that’s that old without completely tearing it down. Not like the people at said hotel were trying that hard. 
##### Fire alarms/other alarms
Some people are oddly comforted by a blinking red or green light going off in their room at random intervals throughout the night. I am not one of those people. It’s something that could theoretically go off at any minute, and if you live in an apartment, you may not even know why initially. When I was a senior in high school, the smoke alarm in our house would go off at random intervals sometimes because a rat got into the attic and shorted some wires. So sometimes I wonder if I would actually sleep MORE soundly in a house without fire alarms in it. Although the next point kind of contradicts this…
##### Lithium batteries
Between the iPad which I’m writing this post on right now, my AirPods and case, my Apple Watch, my phone, and my MacBook, there are no fewer than 7 lithium batteries within 2 feet of me. That’s not comforting. 

Lithium batteries have the tendency to degrade, at which point they usually expand as the lithium reacts and creates a toxic gas within the battery cell. This is usually pretty innocuous, but when these batteries are either overcharged or punctured so that the layers of lithium that are usually separated by a thin, insulating film are touching, the battery enters thermal runaway and starts spewing gas, smoke, and flames before bursting ablaze. Which is exactly what the Samsung Galaxy Note 7 did before it got recalled. 

And people, including myself, sleep with these batteries on them. People have suffered life-altering burns in their sleep. Teslas are pretty much a giant lithium battery. Big yikes.
##### Old arcades
You’re gonna think I hate old stuff. The truth is, I really don’t. There’s plenty of older stuff that rocks. Including arcade games. But there’s just something unsettling about a Chuck E. Cheese or equivalent with all the old, run-down games in it that still have the original CRT. They’re normally empty and they feel like they hold so many memories that wouldn’t be made today that they’re kinda…sad. 

But maybe the main reason for me feeling this way is the fact that arcade games have so many ways you can interact physically with them (which rocks) that sometimes I have nightmares about being locked into an arcade game or an arcade game taking physical control of me the second I touch it. I am one with the machine. But that’s not something you needed to know about me.
##### Tunnels
Finally, tunnels. Same theory as the old building one: how are we sure these things are still good? I go into the metro or any underground tunnel and there’s water dripping from the ceiling. Isn’t water erosive (is that a word?) to rock? Doesn’t it eat away at rock, thereby affecting its structural integrity?  How can you measure the integrity of a tunnel? Probably the one place where I feel the most scared about this is the tunnel under the Potomac between Rosslyn and Foggy Bottom. Because 1. Water weighs a lot and 2. Sometimes trains stop halfway through and 3. I know that if the tunnel breaks and water floods in I’m absolutely cooked. No question about it. 
##### Bonus: Lightbulbs in really high places (yeah, I know I said 5)
I’m not necessarily afraid of heights. I’ll go on a cliff’s edge without trepidation. Balconies? Maybe a bit more freaked out (Not sure why? Cliffs are probably more dangerous?). But the one thing that’s always stumped me is how do you change a lightbulb on a 30 foot ceiling? 

Sometimes I imagine myself doing this and my hands start shaking. What if I fall off the ladder? What if the light bulb breaks and I impale a child with broken glass below? What if the dust from the ceiling falls into my eyes and blinds me? Truly too many variables to feel safe about.
