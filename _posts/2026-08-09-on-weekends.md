Like it or not, the end of summer is quickly approaching. Today is August 9 – in four weeks from tomorrow, Labor Day will descend upon us, marking the cultural end of summer. Our rooftop pool will close. Kids will go back to school. Heck, I’m already seeing back to school ads in malls. School starts in just two weeks!

When I was a kid, I almost always felt like I squandered those halcyon days of summer. Some weren’t my own doing, like when we had to move. Some were, like when I discovered Minecraft in fourth grade. But I always approached the start of each school year with immense dread and resentment towards having to go back to educational institution. 

As an adult who (unfortunately) works a regular job without extended summer breaks, I get a bit of that feeling most weekends. Did I do enough with my weekend? Could I have done anything more? Did I waste my brief respite from corporate America? 

This weekend in particular called that notion into question. Let’s see why...

#### Thursday night
I went out with friends and got…inebriated. Ouch.

#### Friday
I had to call out sick from work with…an illness. Ouch.

#### Friday night
I went to bed at like 9 after sleeping through much of the day due to…reasons.

#### Saturday
Here’s where I turned it around. Woke up feeling better, went to the gym, went and got gas and to the car wash, got a haircut, and then went to a county fair! (See an upcoming post on carnivals and county fairs for more...)

I got home around 11 and went to bed just before midnight. A pretty full day.

#### Sunday
I took care of the essentials: went to the grocery store, went to the car wash AGAIN (I have an unlimited membership so might as well), got some work done I should’ve done Friday, and then went for a good long run. Was happy with myself. Also played some games and then prepared quite the dinner in ye olde crock-pot for tomorrow. Shoutout my crock-pot.

As you can see, my weekend was almost CRIMINALLY jeopardized and in fact extended. I’m not super proud of the fact I went out on a Thursday and felt unwell – as you know, that’s one of my things that was actually out for this summer. Oops.

But all in all, this weekend still managed to be reasonably fun, relaxing, and a good full reset for the week ahead. Yet I wouldn’t have been happy if this was the last weekend of my life. I still feel like there was more to do. Which begs the question...

# How full can a weekend be before it’s no longer fun?
This is an interesting question, and one that could be extended to vacations and holidays in general. I will say that some people are perfectly content with not doing anything over a weekend. Some folks’ ideal weekend is:

Friday – watch a movie at home and order takeout
Saturday – game with friends online
Sunday – sleep in and read a book

Now while that on paper *is* a valid structure for a weekend to take, I’m not convinced I could do it. I love my house and I do enjoy gaming from time to time, but I just feel like (well, at least while it’s above 50 degrees) I have to be outside. And to be fair, it helps living in an area where just walking around is an activity worth noting, and you’ll likely see something new and interesting if you walk around for long enough. 

This does remind me of the time I went to Mexico right after college. We were there for about 9 days and even though there was natural beauty and the resort was awesome, I think we found ourselves pretty much out of ideas, given we shunned the idea of going out to the clubs. (Or I think we did?) So we did do a good job of making our own fun and we definitely took a lot of time to relax, but even there I think I went stir-crazy a bit more than I anticipated. 

And maybe I’m just a fidgety, antsy sort of person. I still like waking up relatively early. As a kid if I didn’t wake up by 6:30 at the latest I would actually get mad at myself for missing part of the day. (This might be part of why I’m good at the whole Corporate America thing.)

But…I have also been ran ragged before by other people on weekends and vacations. It’s almost preferable to going with other people who may slow you down? But also not because you don’t want to be the stick in the mud who just wants to go home while others poke you about the night being young. Which is when a very special technique comes into play…

## An aside on Irishing

Irishing is a very special maneuver that should be used with extreme caution. Better known as “performing an Irish goodbye,” it’s the act of leaving an establishment, location, or get-together you no longer want to attend without providing notice of your departure. Irishing works best when you’re within a stone’s throw of home or some other place you can retreat to easily that’s independent of the other attendees of whatever you are/what you’re at. There are many specific techniques to be used when Irishing from a party or location, but they all fall into one of two categories:

#### Disappearing without a trace
This one’s pretty self-explanatory. This is just sneaking out a front (or back, or side) door (or a window if you’re feeling brave) and disappearing into the night. This one’s a fun one because you’re normally sprinting away from wherever you’re escaping while giggling at being undetected. And its counterpart, which you may need to rely on if caught attempting this method, is...

#### Leaving with an alibi
This one is where creativity comes in. Why are you stepping outside? To go smoke? To let someone in the gate? Why leave? This one definitely garners more suspicion but allows for a greater deal of artistic liberty to be applied, which is why it’s the preferred method of true escape artists. 

All that silly rambling to say that there is a way to cut short an unwelcome part of a weekend if need be. But I don’t think I’ve ever done that to someone who’s dragging out a social gathering, at least on a 1-1 scale or similar. Because that would be very inconsiderate. Doesn’t mean I haven’t thought of it before. But some weekends are just too much excitement. Which is why I’m about to try balancing my perfect weekend (or as close as I can get to one). 

## Creating the ELITE weekend
For this exercise, I’m going to assume the bounds of a standard, two-day weekend, and that I can’t leave work earlier than 2pm on Friday. And that I’m limited to the bounds of a single city, which may or may not be where home is. In this instance and for simplicity’s sake, I’ll try crafting my perfect weekend in and around home. Sounds reasonable, right?

Well, here we go...

## Friday

**3 PM**
We’re leaving work. We’re going home. We’re changing clothes. We’re…going for a run.

You might question this move. Writing this out, I’m questioning this move a little bit too. But bear with me. 

You’re not gonna want to run Saturday morning. You’re not gonna want to run Saturday night. Sunday might be a wash. Get one out of the way early on a Friday! You’ll feel gratified, if not healthier...

**5 PM**
Right, we’ve showered. We’re going to get ready for dinner with the squad. Let’s go somewhere to catch a happy hour dinner deal before it ends at 7. 

**7 PM**
Dinner is over. We go to someone’s house to hang out, maybe play cards, maybe play the Wii, most definitely have a drink or two. This takes us a couple hours, until...

**9:30 PM***
Mobilize. We’re going out. We’re going to the club. There’s music. There’s lights. And there are drinks. But, you will **not** buy a drink. You will watch your friends buy drinks. You will appreciate the way you feel tomorrow after having only had 3-4 drinks. 

**11:30 PM**
You’ve been out a couple hours. You make your exit. Sure, some people stay out later. But you don’t need all that! You go to bed and get a solid night’s rest.

## Saturday
**8 AM** 
You’re up earlier than your friends. You scroll Reels or maybe eat breakfast. That is, before the squad forms back up, and...

**10 AM** 
You hit the (either proverbial or literal, depending on if you’re driving) road. You’re taking an all day excursion somewhere! Maybe you stopped for food right before doing this.

**1 PM**
You’re halfway through whatever you’re doing. You get lunch and sit outside. It’s a pleasantly nice day, probably. If you’re sitting outside and it’s not, that’s on you. 

**4 PM**
The festivities begin to wind down. You start to head back home. You spend a couple hours hanging out, talking, reminiscing, discussing life, and maybe having a couple more drinks. 

**6 PM** 
Some people leave. Others pull out the cards and Wii again and think about going out. They decide to stay home instead.

**11 PM**
Wait, how have 5 hours passed? You were pulling all sorts of shenanigans, from gaming to playing sports outside to going to the store to grab a 24-pack of beer. Somehow, the time’s come to go to bed because everyone’s got things to do tomorrow morning – actual reminders of real-life responsibilities. You settle back down to sleep.

## Sunday
**7 AM**
You wake up not quite refreshed but rested enough. The day is yours. You hydrate, go to the grocery store, and get a workout in in the morning.

**12 PM**
It’s a nice day. You decide to venture outside and walk by the river, stopping at buildings and statues as you pass. There won’t be many days like this left this year before the weather gets cold. You spend a few hours wandering around before heading back home.

**3 PM** 
Your roommates are home and want you to hop on the game. So you do. You win a few games and hop off a couple hours later to eat.

**5 PM** 
You’re all hanging out in the kitchen, cooking and eating and whatnot. You talk about what’s coming up this week. Nothing but work, but you’re feeling ready for it for a change. This weekend really did feel like a complete reset.

**7 PM** 
You catch up on messages, scroll reels, and call your family. Feels good to talk to them again, they’ve missed hearing your voice.

** 10 PM** 
Your alarm is set for 6:30 tomorrow. It’s time to call it a night and a weekend. You ask yourself if this weekend was a good one. It’s a resounding yes.

## Conclusion
Well, I’m pretty happy with that weekend. That’s a pretty common format for mine to take, and I’m always happy to get one like that. Last weekend was a bit more chaotic than that but still super fun. This weekend wasn’t bad all things considered either. I had my fun, and maybe most importantly, I got my rest.
