When we were young, there were always some parts of the world that we formed misconstructions about. We heard misinformation from other kids. We *created* our own misinformation, either intentionally to deceive or as part of a joke taken too literally. Our parents told us tall tales about how the world operated that we believed, because why NOT believe what they say? (Consequently, I no longer believe anything my dad tells me without Googling it.)

But we all certainly believed things as a kid that turned out not to be true. I’m not talking like, big-picture stuff like the Tooth Fairy or Santa Claus or the Easter Bunny being real. I’m talking about stuff like how one could be convinced the [moon was made of cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Grand_Day_Out). We’ve all been told little white lies or we’ve come up with our own incorrect conclusions about the order of the universe. Mostly inconsequential and harmless, save for the brief embarrassment the first time an adult (or one of your peers) corrects you on said topic. And while I feel like mine are silly and I can’t believe I thought them as a kid, we’ve all had some of these incorrect understandings. And those of you who say, “That’s not true of me! I knew how the world worked from the womb!” Well...you're lying. 

So what were the logical fallacies in my head at a young age, you may ask? Well...

## Profanity under 18 is illegal
Now I never cursed as a kid. I still don’t in front of my family. But when my friends did, and even when I said the words “stupid” or “shut up”, my parents were aghast at such a rate that I thought it tantamount to murder. Witnessing the same dismay from my friends’ parents when they cursed, I thought it must have been an actual crime. And especially because I was never given a reason as to why cursing was bad. So clearly there was no other logical explanation than cursing was simply made illegal for kids and their parents didn’t want to have to pick their kids up from jail if they did so in public.

*(Side note: I'm watching Spirited Away while writing this blog and I just got an ad for Skechers with Apple AirTag holes inside for kids who lose their shoes. How stupid of an idea!)*

*(Side side note: I realized this may be helpful to track where your kid is in case anything happens to them WHILE they’re wearing shoes, which makes infinitely more sense and is a much better value proposition.)*

Anyway, what else? 

## Computers and electronics and machines of that sort were sentient (and out to get me)
I don’t know why this is the case, but I felt like things broke more in the 2000s. There was one day our DVD player kept opening and closing on its own, there was a day the security alarm in our house went off for no reason, and it seemed like every day the computer at our house [blue screened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_screen_of_death), scaring the ever loving shit out of me. And me, being the scaredy kid I was, would always startle, jump, and cry when these errors happened. And for whatever reason, I thought it would only happen because computers and machinery hated me SO MUCH that they were out to get me for some reason or another. It got to the point where I would have nightmares about computer errors and getting stuck in rooms and mazes where the walls consisted of cryptic computer errors. Good thing my job doesn’t involve working with computers and the like today...

## Everybody sleepwalked
I don’t know why I thought this, but every time I went to bed I tried setting traps/experiments for myself that would prove, if disturbed, I sleepwalked. I would have lucid dreams where I was in the hallway of our house, and try to wake myself up. I never managed to wake myself up while still standing. I would pretend to sleepwalk and annoy my parents. And when I woke up after tossing and turning, voilá – of COURSE the sheets were disturbed and I was like, “Well that proves it, I sleepwalk.” That never really turned out to be the case, but I can’t remember my parents denying the whole sleepwalking thing, so maybe it was partially their fault.

## Teachers couldn’t have children
Maybe this is **partially** true to some extent, and people with a love for children (not like the panel van and free candy kind of love) become teachers because they can’t/don’t want to have any of their own. But up until fourth grade, I don’t think any of my teachers had kids. They probably did but they never talked about it. All I know is that some of my teachers, like my very evil second grade teacher who probably deserves her own blog post, wouldn’t have made for very good parents in my head. 

*(Another side note: my second grade teacher is now a pastor with her own Christian school and program. Good for her? Even if she was mean to 7-year-old me.)*

## Teenagers didn’t have parents (and were paid to join childless families)
THIS was the impetus to writing this blog. This oddly-held belief right here. 

I could have sworn that there was some sort of job board, or page in the newspaper, where teenagers were just sort of orphans you could hire. They could babysit, they could work in fast food, or they could…live in your house and cause chaos. 

Our next-door neighbors as a kid had teenagers. They were pretty typical 2008 teenagers – flip phones, AOL, MySpace, Skype…and they were only a little mean to the neighborhood kids. But for some reason I just never figured they were our neighbors’ children. Maybe because at that age, I never foresaw myself becoming a teenager. Maybe I was just too dumb to realize I was growing up? 

But I think it would be interesting if there WAS a teenager-for-hire job board and you could just…pay teenagers to live at your house. Who would do it? I don’t know, probably nobody. Why would they do it? Hell if I know. But it’s an interesting idea to think about.

## Flights weren’t scheduled unless enough people wanted to go to the same place
I thought that as a kid if you had a flight booked, you went to the airport and if not enough people wanted to fly to the same place as you that day, they canceled the flight. Imagine if that’s how things actually worked! It would feel pretty bad if you booked a flight to a place that nobody wanted to go to. I would be embarrassed, frankly, if nobody wanted to go to the same place as me. Thanking my lucky stars that’s NOT how it works.

## Divorces meant nobody got custody
Once again, the whole orphans thing. I’m not sure if it’s media or what but I was under the assumption as a kid that way more people were orphans than was actually the case. 

I’ll preface this one with the somber fact that unfortunately, I’m sure in some divorces, neither parent gets custody because neither is fit to take care of their children. But I thought it was every divorce. Maybe I didn’t pay enough attention to stepmoms and stepdads to the point I thought every kid with divorced parents had both a stepmom AND stepdad and they lived together and they just…adopted the kid? I really don’t know on this one.

## I had no idea what drinking and driving meant
Finally, I thought very wrong on what drinking and driving meant. I thought to get in trouble for drinking and driving, a cop had to catch you drinking at the same time as you were driving. So theoretically, you COULD have 10 beers at the bar and get in and drive home perfectly fine. On the flip side, you could drink a water bottle at a stop light and a cop could arrest you for that. This was actually something dispelled pretty quickly by my parents. Probably because I chided them every time they drank water in the car while driving and I thought they were going to get in trouble. 

But anyway, I swear I believe none of these things 18 or 20 or so years on. I was an impressionable kid, as many are, and I bet you were too.
