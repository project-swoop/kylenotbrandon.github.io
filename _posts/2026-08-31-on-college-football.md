Now I HATE the idea of Twitter burners. They all sort of follow a certain format: have a random [FIRST_NAME] “[NICKNAME]” [LAST_NAME] handle, take random pictures harassing strangers at bars, and comment on obscure fraternity pledges doing silly stuff. But every once in a while, you get a gem like the above. And whoever’s behind the Hugh Janus account is *probably* a terrible person, but they did cook on this singular tweet.

I don’t know about you, but I’m tired of the slopification of college football. The game used to mean something more – young men playing their hearts out for the glory of their alma maters, with 40,000 of their classmates PACKING the stands. Local rivalries. Conference-versus-conference debates. Hokey traditions that made no sense for the singularly-televised game each year, but meant everything to the people who lived and breathed their team. College football was at its best when it didn’t *mean* anything bigger than what it was. It didn’t pretend to have the polish of the NFL. It didn’t need millions of dollars to create die-hard fans or immortalize legends of the sport. 

So where did it all start going wrong?

## Sin #1: Athlete over student
Have you ever driven past an old building where something like a store you loved going to, or your favorite restaurant as a kid, or perhaps a Chuck E. Cheese used to stand, and felt the emotion of not being able to go back abut being glad you were able to experience it? You can always buy yourself a pizza (and probably a nicer one), but you’ll never be able to pull a soggy, heat lamp-warmed slice of pizza off the [Souplantation](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2020-05-07/souplantations-buffet-restaurants-closing-coronvirus) buffet line ever again. 

In a similar vein, you’ll never be able to experience college football players NOT getting paid ever again. Am I destroyed over it? Not particularly. But am I happy that I have to buy a $30 pizza to get the same effect as Souplantation – I mean…have to watch college kids walk out of their school on a whim because Lane Kiffin is offering $1.5 million and a G-Wagon? Not particularly either.

I *do* think college athletes, especially in risky, high-injury contact sports like football deserve to be paid. Especially when universities are making millions upon billions for their likenesses – and are SO hesitant to invest their money anywhere. It was a travesty that athletes would be stars for 3-4 years in college and then suffer an injury and lose out on tens of millions of dollars in career earnings to have to go work in insurance sales. But the fact that NIL in its current state lacks all forms of regulation is equally irresponsible. 

In one way, we’re not setting these young athletes up to succeed. The transfer portal lights up like never before these days. It’s no longer about playing time, or winning championships, or even necessarily putting oneself in the best position to go pro. It’s about revenue at a level when it shouldn’t be **about** revenue.

We’re also being unfaithful to our student-athletes by frequently eschewing the student part of their commitments. As part of these NIL deals, and these never-ending transfers, “students" often have their work done for them by tutors, do not/never pursue degrees, and then in recourse have no viable path to a future aside from coming back to college on their own dime if the NFL doesn’t come calling. Stetson Bennett famously spent SIX years in college and did not receive a degree. And now, he’s an NFL free agent, who may never get another call. Good luck? (He’s one of the lucky ones with the Rams holding onto him for that long.)

Money has these players focused on the WRONG goals. At every possible level, it’s a cash grab for student-athletes. Because how certain *can* one be about a future in pro sports? It’s almost required at that point for one to be short-sighted. But the fact we’ve allowed unregulated NIL spending is sport-altering in a way I can’t call palatable, and it doesn’t seem like a salary cap/regulations would be palatable to players either.

## Sin #2: Nationalization
Conferences in college used to be SO fun. Everybody remembers the old [Big 10 intro](https://youtu.be/TILStesGd4g?si=9qWDBEunSDzzFwzt), or the [SEC on CBS intro](https://youtu.be/I1lmUIF1odU?si=lZ8Wglrp6N9aiigo&t=102)…both a few shining examples (of many) that really demonstrated a bit of the college football landscape you couldn’t get anywhere else. 

And I get why, especially in the 12-team CFP era where more teams have the chance to win the national championship every year, you would want to play in stronger conferences. And not to say that there aren’t bottom-feeders in conferences these days, because there still are. But we’ve lost great rivalry games to nationalization (Oklahoma vs. Oklahoma State, Oregon vs. Oregon State, Michigan vs. Notre Dame) because everyone’s playing in these massive conferences where head-to-head records matter SO MUCH that you need to make that conference championship game (and usually win) to guarantee yourself a playoff spot. 

So there is a case for it. But there are the lost touches of magic. There are the dormant rivalries. And there are completely geographically incoherent conferences. 

Stanford and Cal in the *Atlantic Coast Conference*??? Washington, Oregon, USC, and UCLA in the Big Freakin’ 10??? The temporary death of the Pac-12? 

I’m sure we’ll come around to new rivalries and this new way of the world soon enough, but it’s absolutely BRUTAL that so much of the college football landscape has changed in so little time. Like a house-flipper buying your childhood home of 18 years and turning it all millennial grey in 6 months. 

## Sin #3: The Disney Effect

*(Side note: Before I dug into this section, I erroneously thought Disney took over ESPN programming in like 2017, and not in 1996. But it still doesn’t change the fact Disney’s creative direction is at fault for the gripes I’m about to describe.)*

This REALLY gets to the crux of slopification of the college football product. Aside from the mercuriality of players driven by the dollar over glory, and the Super Duper Mega Conference Extraordinaires™️, it’s the presentation of the game that’s really gone down the drain.

And I fault ESPN for spearheading it, in many ways. As I’m sure many of you know, ESPN produces (what used to be, at least) the crown jewel of all college football programming in College GameDay. And it used to be glorious. Drunken college students, fun signs, the crew of Chris, Kirk, Desmond, Coach Corso, and The Bear…it was great. Nonstop coverage of pregame situations ALL across the country with a live audience. The iconic headgear pick. Celeb picks. Yet above all else, people were there for the football.

Then something changed. I’m not sure if it was audience-driven (because people can’t sit through 3 hours of anything anymore), if it was a corporate decision, or if it was solely because Pat McAfee joined the show, but things have felt so…gimmicky since. 

I’m speaking from firsthand experience here. I’ve BEEN in the audience for College GameDay. And it was NOT fun.

There was a line, yes, and there was a team of entertainers there hyping the crowd up…but it started SO awkwardly.

College GameDay is now preceded by the Pat McAfee Show. Which is the biggest joke of a mashup of pop culture, TikTok reactions, and very little talk about actual football. Complete with cutaways to Pat’s cronies like the ever-talkative A.J. Hawk and the very reasonable Aaron Rodgers. And it’s so finance bro-esquely commercialized through Barstool ads and betting ads and God knows what else that it’s unbearable. Never mind the commercial breaks.

But a lot of this comes through on GameDay as well. Pat’s yelling as loud as he can for no apparent reason. The other hosts are getting more and more drawn into his antics year by year. It’s wholly incoherent as a show now, and it feels like they’re doing a LOT of work to keep the whole production on track. It’s bloated, it’s dumbed-down, and similarly to the Pat McAfee Show, it’s all content farming for clips. Not that I’m against a kid kicking a football for the chance at free tuition, of course.

But all this commercialization does make me reflect on what we had, and how precious it was. Dang it, I don’t care if it was a fundamentally flawed sport. I’d take the BCS computer from 1998 spitting out random numbers to decide a national champion again. I’d take back the awkward TV blackouts. I’d even take back Nick Saban.

But as I say, part of something being able to be appreciated is the fact that you can’t have it forever. And while I miss those days, I don’t think they’d be nearly as special if they stuck around forever. So goodbye, prime college football. I’ll think of you when I tune into the Polymarket Palantir Flock Labubu Bowl presented by Crumbl on ESPN3+ this January.
