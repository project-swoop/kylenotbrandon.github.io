I'm going to speak for a lot of men when I say this. Or so I think.

There are a lot of things I - and we - enjoy that we're afraid to admit. We spend our lives concerned about others' perception of us and our interests, and in the process, ignore things we actually love and care about. 

It *is* true that some people have guilty pleasures that are more 'pled guilty in court' pleasures. See: Jeffrey Dahmer. To set the record straight, I'm NOT talking about those. I'm talking about the things we're sometimes embarrassed to say we like. Things you wouldn't say to your boss or on a first date but things you would say to your friends. Things that force you to be vulnerable to disclose. But once again, not in like an incriminating way.

I got the inspiration to write this because I've seen a lot of content from the fine folks at TMZ. Because lately, TMZ has descended onto the streets of Washington, DC and called the men here boring. Well, only a subsection of men. That I happen to belong to.

Rep. Ritchie Torres, you may have some poor political positions but you're right about one thing: these men are boring. 

And I'm almost convinced some of these people (which my friend group refers to as DMV bots) lack a personality because they have no guilty pleasures. They're so mainstream and aggressively milquetoast that the most adventurous thing they've done is go to a state fair once. Do I give off that impression? Maybe. But we're all being judged, so maybe I can't judge.

But in the hope that these people DO have more intriguing facets internally we can eke out of each other by being mutually vulnerable, I decided I'd make myself vulnerable first and list some of my guilty pleasures. If **you** want to share your guilty pleasures to avoid my boring allegations, feel free to do so at [kylenotbrandon@gmail.com](mailto:kylenotbrandon@gmail.com).

## My guilty pleasures

You know what, I'm hoping only people I can trust read this blog. If people outside of my immediate friends are reading this, I hope you don't care enough to identify me by this. In either case, here we go, roughly in order of least to most embarrassing...

#### Cats (over dogs)

Everyone who knows me knows I really like cats. And some day, I will have a cat. I hope. But until then, I'm going to keep going over to people's homes. And if they have a cat, I will bug their cat. I will pick up their cat. Because when I'm not visiting my parents and seeing our cat, I'm severely cat-deprived.

#### Baking

This isn't a bad one either. Well, maybe for my health? I love baking. I love little sweet treats. Baking (a set and forget activity) is infinitely easier than cooking, which requires far more prep, far more attention, far more care...which is also why I love my Crock-Pot. 

#### Yearning music

Actually, maybe this one is the least guilty. I, as many people do (and as work has given me more of lately), have bad days. There are scientific studies that say listening to sad, wistful, or yearnful (is that a word? My editor isn't giving it a red squiggly line) music actually helps you feel better because it validates your feelings. Is it a stark departure from the higher-energy stuff I normally listen to? Yes. But sometimes it's good to listen to *All I Need* by Radiohead and stare out the window at the street. I swear.

#### Urban architecture

It's so fascinating to me how we even got to the point of 10-story buildings. How we went from ornate to brutalist to postmodern and all the designs in between. How we plan our cities out to be spaces where we're (well, usually) designing for humans to flourish. 

I like to think that cities are one of the most mindfully designed places on Earth. They are places where hundreds of thousands of people are meant to coexist and live together. And there's something very cool about that. And living in a city, we're given things to do and places to see and statues to climb on top of, which is totally always encouraged and you totally should do to aura farm for your Instagram. 

From earlier blogs, we all know my disdain for suburbs. But sometimes I do feel bad at how suburbs aren't designed with nearly the same thought and care for the people living in them as cities. But then again, I always say I lack credibility. Reading this blog, you should have established that already.

#### Mechanical keyboards

I love mechanical keyboards because they're sort of a return to quality in a product! Are they consumerist? A bit. But I also appreciate watches, and colognes, and shoes. Which are all a bit more fast fashion and disposable. I mean, perfumes are literally consumable items. Shoes break down and wear out after an amount of time or wear. Watches get bumped around and come in and out of fashion. But there is something special about a mechanical keyboard. It's modular, it feels great, it sounds great, it's high-quality, and they're actually getting cheaper as more vendors come out with their own offerings. The mechanical keyboard I'm typing on right now is at least 4 years old and feels the exact same as it did when I bought it. They're awesome! Get one if you're a serious typer.

#### Trucks

I'm morally torn on people who drive trucks. And maybe so because I've been shamed for driving a truck. Yes, it's uneconomical. Yes, it pollutes the air. And yes, it does have design flaws that make it more dangerous than the normal vehicle. But it's cool! I love them. I've always wanted one since I was a kid. It is the epitome of America to drive around in a truck! So I do feel a bit guilty because they are antithetical to my love for the environment and the outdoors, being so inefficient. But they're damn cool and I'll stand by that.

#### Mayonnaise

I just love a sandwich with mayonnaise on it. 

I HATE eggs. I can barely eat an egg. But mayonnaise? An egg-based condiment? I'll dip my fries in it, I'll order it on my sandwich...I'll put it on a lot of stuff. Popular Chicago-based hot dog chain Portillo's puts mayonnaise in their cake! And it's good! So if you see me with a bottle of mayonnaise, mind your business.

#### Rom-coms

This is where it starts getting worse...

Any time I go home, I sit in the living room with my mom. And she's watching some cheesy rom-com or some TV show of that nature. And I ALWAYS get sucked into watching. I'll ask questions about the plot. I'll ask questions about the characters. And sometimes if there's an emotional scene I'll end up tearing up with my mom. Oops.

#### Fortnite

Nobody is playing Fortnite these days. I know I'm playing with like, 9-year-olds. But damn, if it isn't fun and damn, if I'm not good at it. Enough said there.

#### Girlypop music

I feel like all people can appreciate girlypop music. My big 3 are Carly Rae Jepsen (the best thing to come out of Canada), Charli XCX, and Lorde. I will tear up to *Supercut*. I do know every word to *Run Away With Me*. I get sad to *Track 10*. But it's just SO good. 

I think the song that's really capped the genre off for me lately...well there's two. One is a bit more downtempo and yearning-esque, as per the above category. And that's Backseat by Charli XCX feat. Carly Rae Jepsen.

#### Stuffed animals

Allow me to rant for a brief second. I've been trying to be brief for brevity's sake, because I've been writing this for an hour and a half already, but just give me this. 

I loved stuffed animals as a kid. My first was a stuffed Winnie the Pooh, and the first one I ever picked out was a tiger. 

But at a certain point, my mom decided I was too old for stuffed animals, and gave my veritable collection away. 

Was I too old? I don't know, I was like 13. But I was immature then and I'm immature now.

But what irked me is that I'm a deeply sentimental person. That was HARD, especially as a kid. But my mom kept her stuffed animals from when she was a kid in a box in her closet. They're still in her closet. So as a form of rebellion and self-expression, when I went to college, I bought a stuffed alligator. And that stuffed alligator is about 8 feet to my right in my room right now. 

I have a couple in my bed. It's just one of those things that remind me we were all children at some point, we all should approach the world with a sense of childlike wonder, and that we're never too old to seek comfort in what's a tough world. And in my heart, we're all children until the day we die. So kids, don't grow up.

And I felt really validated that Kenzie from Love Island, who we should ALL take as a role model, slept with her stuffed rabbit every night on the show. Truly inspirational.
