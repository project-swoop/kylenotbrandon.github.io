title: A letter to my cat
excerpt: (On loss)

I was 9 when I met you. I’m 24 now. 

I wouldn’t have ever had gotten to know you if, on the 25 minute drive to breakfast one weekend in San Diego, my mom hadn’t needed to stop and use the bathroom. You know, we had two cats before you, and when they passed in 2006 and 2009, my mom proclaimed she was done and would never have another cat as long as she lived. 

But when we pulled into that shopping center with the PetSmart right next to the store with the bedding in it, I knew I had to drag my dad inside, all the way to the cat enclosure next to the bathrooms. Which is where I saw you for the first time!

I’d wanted another cat ever since our last one had passed three years prior. Our dog at the time, as you quickly became aware of, was MEAN. Really mean. And I wasn’t quite fond of her. So when I walked in and looked into the cages and saw your little black-and-white face, I was enthralled. You were 10 months old at the time and looked back at me with as much curiosity as you saw in my own expression. You leaned against the cage door so I could pet you through the bars. You were always a people pleaser like that.

But alas, I was dragged back to the car, and we went to breakfast that day. But I couldn’t stop thinking about you. 

Somehow I convinced my parents to go back. And that time you weren’t there. I was devastated. My mother tried comforting me saying that you’d gone to a loving home of your own, and that you were going to live a happy life. But then an old lady from the cat rescue walked in, and told my mom that you’d be back next week. At that point I knew you had my mom’s heart too. 

So we went back the next week. You were there. My parents thought about it, and talked about it, and we took you home. I stayed in the cat enclosure scratching you and you reached a little black-and-white paw through the bars and swatted at my hat. The couple in there thought it was the sweetest thing. I had to tell them you were coming home with me, of course. 

We bought a cat tree, a litter box, food, toys, and made our way back home. That was one of the happiest days of my life. Hopefully it was for you, too. Until you met our dog.

Our dog was 14 and MEAN. She was too damn old for another annoyance in the house. So she chased you behind my parents’ bed. I cried. My parents laughed. I had no idea what was going on, but she didn’t mind you too much in the end. 

I remember my friends coming over and having fun playing with you. I remember when they’d tell me you were the first cat they liked, and the first one they weren’t scared of. I remember the year we spent in Rhode Island, where you were fascinated by the snow. You’d run out under the pine trees and play in the snow, and we’d chase you around to try getting you back inside after you dashed out. I remember trying to stay up for New Year’s with my dad, and you’d fall asleep on top of me and I’d end up falling asleep too. I remember my mom and I teaching you how to sit and shake. I felt so accomplished that we were able to teach you that. 

Every time I was sick, you were there. Every time I was sad, you were there. Every time I was happy, you were there. You loved to be around people! When a repairman came by, you came right up to him for attention. You made everyone you interacted with happier. Including myself, of course.

But we couldn’t all be young forever. You were 8 when I went to college. I’d always see you when I came back. You wouldn’t play as much. We’d sleep on the couch together. I’d let you sleep in my bed at night, which my mom objected to heavily. When I graduated, you were 12. But you were still the same sweet cat. You’d lay on my chest, purring up a storm, and Mom would always take embarrassing pictures of us laying there together. And you got to be the mean pet when my parents got a puppy when I was in college!

But you slowed down, and the bloodwork showed signs of kidney failure. Kidney failure gets a lot of cats. It was manageable at first – we bought you special food, and you seemed alright. But nothing lasts forever.

Last year I came home for Thanksgiving. You were skinnier, older, less fur. You were still you though. 

You’d stumble, and cry when you got lost. You’d clamor to be picked up and loved being held. I think that was your way of saying you were scared of what was happening. But no matter what, you’d always love laying on the couch with me when I came home. Inside you were always the same cat.

Mom and I called every week. You weren’t feeling well. You’d often hide. You’d seem down. And we were worried about you. You’d get better, and worse, and sometimes it was 1 step forward, 2 steps back, and occasionally vice versa.

I’m really sorry that the last couple weeks were hard for you. You’d throw up, or make a mess of the litterbox, and go hide for the rest of the day. You were in pain, and you were miserable. It was devastating having to hear what you were going through.

More than anything, I’m really sorry I couldn’t have been there to see you one more time. You tried your best to hold out. I come home on Sunday. You passed on Tuesday. Which was probably for the best. No protracted suffering, no super-long medical emergency...

But I’ll never get to see you again. And that’s painful. I hoped and prayed for it, but life doesn’t always work that way.

Regardless, I’m glad that you went peacefully. I’m glad you got 15 damn good years of life. I’m glad I got to know and love you, and I’m glad, selfishly, that you came home with us. And I’m glad my Mom’s bladder can’t make it more than 15 minutes without needing a bathroom. 

Thank you for all the joy. Thank you for swatting at the bill of my hat 15 years ago in the PetSmart. Thank you for being so loving in my hardest moments. Thank you for the memories. Thank you for all the love when I was sick or down, or angry. Thank you for all the nights on the couch. Thank you. You’ll forever leave a mark on my heart.
