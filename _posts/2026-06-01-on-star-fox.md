For so long, Nintendo has been the undisputed GOAT of accessible gaming experiences. The NES revolutionized gaming at home. The N64 brought a certain whimsy to consoles that Sony, Atari, and Sega weren’t at the time. The Game Boys brought handheld gaming to the forefront and made car rides and department store lines 1000 time better. The DS was an icon. The Switch is the standard for modular, convertible gameplay. And the Wii, oh my goodness the Wii – it speaks volumes that the Wii is still being played in the living room in our home 20 years on. (Let’s just not talk about the Wii U.)

While Nintendo does other stuff like Mii-based games and is a peddler of titles like Pokémon and Kirby, those are 1. system-dependent in the case of Miis and 2. co-owned/produced by external companies in the case of Pokémon and Kirby. But it’s a so-called “Big 5” franchises that have kept Nintendo in the spotlight and adored by families for decades:

- Mario (duh)
- The Legend of Zelda
- Animal Crossing
- Splatoon
- Pikmin

These series are recognizable for their bright, colorful, polished graphics – and they’re all stunning. Breath of the Wild was incredible even on the lower-powered hardware of the Switch 1. Yet with one notable surprise as of late, maybe they’re trying to change their image in an attempt to make it a Big 6…
## Enter Star Fox

Maybe this was a bit of a coincidence in timing rather than a purely strategic decision, but in *The Super Mario Galaxy Movie*, hardcore Nintendo fans (which admittedly I am not one of) were surprised to see a familiar face appear in Fox McCloud, from the mildly popular game series *Star Fox*. Now I asked Google to summarize the series in one sentence and got this:

**Star Fox follows an elite squad of anthropomorphic animals, led by Fox McCloud, who pilot futuristic Arwing spacecraft and battle the evil scientist Andross to protect the Lylat planetary system.** 

Pretty spot on.

![Fox McCloud in the Super Mario Galaxy Movie](https://kylenotbrandon.blog/assets/img/IMG_0262.PNG)

Now I haven’t watched the movie, but his cameo was brief and his character, voiced by Glen Powell, felt…off. Fox feels too chipper, too animated, and too polished for a guy who’s spent 9 games being a stoic 20-something trying to save the universe almost single-handedly. He’s supposed to be serious and cool and neither of those came through in the movie adaptation, unfortunately. And admittedly, I’ve spent such little time paying attention to this movie that I wasn’t aware of the cameo until well after release. And when I did, I was disappointed that they stripped away the character of the…character (ugh, I know) and was worried for the future of the game series. Until…

## Star Fox on the Switch 2

This is just a brief part of the game’s prologue, but it looks AWESOME. They nailed everything. You don’t need a space war to be polished and shiny. It *should* be brutal and dark and a little moody. Even if it’s fighting with lasers and energy weapons and sci-fi stuff, you can tell these characters aren’t smiling through it all. But there is one thing that stands out to me.  

The characters…
![Fox and friends](https://kylenotbrandon.blog/assets/img/IMG_0264.PNG)

They look a little weird. There’s a bit of an uncanny valley where you can have some human traits like whites in eyes and human hands and legs and speech patterns, but the designers over at Nintendo seem to have overbaked this a little. The animal legs are one offender, as are the tiny little eyes that I think would look better without the whites. These characters don’t feel expressive or nearly as visually endearing as older versions with bigger eyes and human legs. See 2018’s *Starlink: Battle for Atlas* as an example:

![Fox and friends, 8 years ago](https://kylenotbrandon.blog/assets/img/IMG_0266.JPG)

See? Personally I wish they had gone for a bit more of that design. While I appreciate and love the realism and I’m imagining they’re getting every bit out of the Switch 2’s (ostensibly limited, I don’t own one) hardware, it’s a bit poorly shod.

Probably the worst offender is Fox’s dad James, who’s shown in the prologue I linked above:

![$50 on pump 8 and a roller dog](https://kylenotbrandon.blog/assets/img/IMG_0265.JPG)

They made him a chud! This man was there on January 6th and he’ll be back for Freedom 250 on the Mall! He has every red hat ever made!

I’m not sure why they made the character’s necks look like that (because it’s not like any of the characters are overly large). But they’re definitely weirdly proportioned to the point that I’m, as you may be able to tell, not a fan. But the gameplay looks so good and I’m so intrigued by it that it might be worth buying a Switch 2 to judge it for myself. ## Are Star Fox chuds?

This is a good argument for several reasons. One, the team has gone through a significant change in composition between the original version with James and the edition Fox leads in the actual games. Pigma defected. But Star Fox is by definition a private military company, so seems pretty chudlike to me. 

Here’s my succinct analysis of this sitation:

On the original team, James (Fox’s dad) is probably around the age to be a Baby Boomer and paid about $16 for a house new in the 80s. If the aviators and the look doesn’t do it, it’s probably the steadfast determination to serve the establishment that motivated him more than the moral obligation to defeat evil. Probably a chud.

Pigma (who defected to the enemy team, Star Wolf) is the biggest chud in the universe. You can tell he’s there for money and money alone, and since master chud Andross has all the money in the world of course Pigma capitulated to him. Actually, all of Star Wolf are probably chuds (except for Panther who is the resident womanizer of the game series and would go along with whatever girl he’s talking to says). 

Peppy Hare, the third and final member of the original team, is an interesting read. He has a southern accent which indicates he’s a chud, but I feel like he’s too caring to be a chud. Also, hanging out around 20-something year olds as part of the new team is (hopefully?) radicalizing in the direction away from being a chud.

That brings us to the new team. Naturally we’ll start with Fox.

Fox presumably grew up in/around Corneria City, which seems pretty liberal. He’s educated (I think?). He’s the kind of coastal elite who would never outwardly express it but grimace inside if you said you were from Louisiana.

Falco Lombardi (the blue bird who’s maybe the best pilot on the team but is a loudmouth) is so clearly from New Jersey and so clearly voted for Donald Trump (or the space equivalent) so his taxes would go down. Chud that would take offense to the term.

And Slippy Toad doesn’t know how to register to vote. Bro spends all day on his computer and either games, builds, or flies, he doesn’t know about politics. But he would be so scared to be called a chud if you pressed him on it that he’d shy away from chud politics anyway.

## In conclusion

I think writing this blog has taught me that my best work does not come after a couple drinks. But in summary, I’m excited to see Nintendo go a new direction with something that’s not exactly adult by nature or violent but darker, more detailed, and a little more heavy than another shiny, ultrapolished Nintendo staple. I’m hoping this can unlock a bigger audience for more new games to come. And maybe I will buy that Switch 2.
