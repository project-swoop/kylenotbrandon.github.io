## A background on humility

I was prompted to think of this topic when I thought about how everyone in DC (where I live, thereabouts) comes off so...distant. I feel like I have a hard time relating to people from certain places (and sometimes, in general) but people in DC...especially so. This is NOT an indictment of DC - because I realize that in the majority of these cases, the people I'm referring to are not from the area themselves (neither am I). But DC tends to attract characters I would have a hard time calling humble for some reason. And in the same vein, I wonder how humble I am myself.

But what *is* humility? Our old pal Google says, "Humility is the quality of having a modest and accurate view of one's own importance, coupled with a freedom from pride and arrogance." Some examples provided include:

- Graciously receiving criticism and acting upon it rather than making excuses

- Admitting mistakes rather than passing blame

- Asking for help from others

- Giving others credit for successes

- Actively listening to understand others

- Letting go of the need to be right over differing opinions/minor arguments

- Apologizing first

- Living a simple, unpretentious lifestyle and remaining grounded

- Being willing to support others/do the menial work without expecting praise in return

- Celebrating others without feeling insecure

I also looked for antitheses of humility, and found these:

- **Hubris** - exaggerated, dangerous pride that blinds people into their own downfall

- **Arrogance** - self-importance and superiority to the extent of treating others as less valuable

- **Self-righteousness** - the belief that your actions are flawlessly correct, while others are always wrong

- **Narcissism** - This one hardly needs an explanation.

- Claiming solo praise for a team effort

- Defensiveness/anger when given constructive criticism

- Condescension/talking down to peers, friends, or acquaintances

- Needing to outdo someone else when they share an achievement (one-upmanship)

- Believing you're entitled to special treatment or getting out of things others are expected to do

So that was probably an overly verbose explanation of how the book defines humility. But why do I call DC out in particular for the observed lack of humility? Well...I have my theories. One is that this is the case with any city that's more metropolitan. Because I did get this impression when I went to London last year, and from Paris, and from what I hear out of New York, it's the same way. Hell, SoCal (my homeland) is known for this (although I find this to be a very small contingent of folks). So maybe it's a general city thing and I'm taking the easy way out by picking on DC. But I *am* confronted with it daily here, so it's rubbed me the wrong way by volume much more here. 

Another theory is that it's simply a product of vocation, and the DC hyperfocus on tying one's career status to one's worth. "This is what I do and why it's so important to society and everyone should TOTALLY care about it. Never mind I'm doing the same thing as the other 700,000 people that live here!" Some people are probably playing the game in this way because that's what they've seen. It's a very transactional school of thought, but unfortunately a prevalent one here. And it's easy to go along with. I speak about this a LOT. Unfortunately, we so rarely get to work a job that genuinely makes an impact on the world. But that won't stop people in DC, especially, from boasting that they do. Sorry, wrong.

At this point you're probably thinking the same thing as I am: Man, this guy doesn't have a humble bone in his body. And I see your point. But I'm convinced that these are thoughts everyone has. I tend not to share them in real life (does this count? am i contradicting myself here?), but I'm convinced most people have them. And philosophers across the ages have long claimed that our thoughts are not what makes us - rather, it's our actions. You can have unruly passengers on your plane, but they're (usually, and hopefully) not going to steer you off course. 

So in the concluding two sections of this article, I'm going to introduce to you my brand-new, open-source framework for determining how humble I, you, or anyone out there really are. Then, I'm going to score myself (and then we'll see).

## The Big, Unified, Labeled List of Scientific Humility Investigation Tests (B.U.L.L.S.H.I.T)

Welcome to B.U.L.L.S.H.I.T! This list of 10 scenario-based, yes/no questions will certainly determine how humble you truly are from an empirical perspective. This battery of questions was peer-reviewed by nobody and should not be trusted by anybody. But here we go anyway. Count up how many yeses you answer with, you'll need that number:

1. Do you ever doubt that you're a humble person/others perceive you as humble?

2. Your manager applauds you for a job well-done on a difficult problem at work. You've been struggling recently, and in reality, your coworker Carl came up with and implemented the solution. Do you tell your manager Carl was the one who deserves the credit?

3. You're working at a hardware store in the paint department. A little old lady comes up to you and asks you where the brass fittings for bathroom sinks are. You don't know. Do you apologize and tell her you don't know (yes), or confidently make up a guess that may be wrong (no)? 

4. You're sitting at a red light and looking down at your phone. The light turns green, and after about a second the car behind you starts laying on the horn. Do you apologize with a wave (yes), or do you grow irritated with them (no)?

5. It's your first week at a new job, and you've been asked to create a slide deck, referring to this notes drive. You don't catch where it was because you weren't paying full attention. Do you follow up with a quick chat message apologizing and asking where the drive was again (yes), or try figuring it out on your own (no)?

6. You're at the train station and you're at the gates as your train is pulling up. As you start rushing to the stairs, an old man stops you and asks you for directions. Do you stop to give him directions, knowing the next train isn't for another 10 minutes?

7. You've had a bad day and go out to eat. The restaurant you choose is packed - it's a miracle you've even gotten a table. The waiter offers a halfhearted apology, but the service is still slow. Do you still thank them and tip them appropriately?

8. You’re in a meeting and confidently suggest an approach. A junior teammate politely points out a flaw in your logic, and after thinking about it, you realize they’re right. Do you openly acknowledge that they were right and adjust your view?

9. You've just finished dinner at an acquaintance's house. They've spent 2 hours on it and it was magnificent. Everyone else heads home but you head to the bathroom before you leave. When you get out, you see the host start doing the dishes. You're tired, but you can see they're more tired than you are. You offer to help, and they politely say, “No, no, you’re a guest.” You can tell they’re only saying that to be polite and are exhausted. Do you insist and help anyway (yes), or do you accept the out and go like everyone else (no)?

10. You're on the way home from a 10-hour day at work. Some charity has set up a booth on the street corner. You're tired and you don't want to talk to anyone, but they specifically call you out. Do you engage with them and hear what they have to say (yes), or just ignore them (no)?

**SCORING INSTRUCTIONS:** Each yes answer should be counted as a point. Divide the number of points you receive by the total number of questions (10) and multiply by 100 to receive your Humility Percentage.

## How I did

Question 5 - I would say I did a poor job writing this question. I would ask it, but more in a "hey, how do I spell that?" way. In that vein, I'm saying that's a no because I'm not exactly telling the truth.

Question 6 - Unfortunately this has worked against me before and I have done this, so yes. I'm a sucker for helping people out even if it slows me down.

Question 7 - Yes. I know how much a retail job can suck, and that two people can be having bad days.

Question 8 - Yes...I feel like this specifically happens to me. I am not quite a 10x engineer yet.

Question 9 - Yes, once again because I have been said host at times and it's NOT fun. My pro tip is don't host events.

Question 10 - No. Nope. Those people know what they're doing. They exist to piss you off and reel suckers in. I cross the *street* to avoid them.

All in all, by the B.U.L.L.S.H.I.T metric, looks like I'm 70% humble. Could be worse.
